# Research: alternative information sources — 2026-07-13

Context: eight technical price-action configurations (momentum x3, mean
reversion x2, breakout, trend following x2) have failed walk-forward
validation across this and prior sessions. Per `WALKFORWARD_RESULTS.md`,
that hypothesis space is closed. This document reviews whether a
**different information source** — social sentiment, news sentiment, or
congressional trading disclosures — has credible, documented,
retail-implementable edge after realistic costs. Sources were read
skeptically; promotional and content-marketing material is flagged as
such.

## Verdict, up front

**Nothing found tonight clears the bar to build.**

1. **Social/news sentiment momentum: do not build.** The best recent
   academic backtest of news-sentiment signals finds returns are eroded
   or eliminated by transaction costs; the celebrated positive results
   come from promotional sources, cost-free backtests, or the 2020-21
   meme regime that has since decayed. This is the same "no edge after
   honest costs" pattern our own eight failures showed, one layer up.
2. **Congressional disclosure copying: do not build now.** The
   peer-reviewed post-STOCK-Act literature finds members of Congress in
   aggregate have **no** stock-picking alpha — House members' buys
   *underperform* by ~26bp over 6 months — and any documented alpha
   (pre-2012, or the 2025 leadership-subset result) accrues to the
   politicians **at trade time**, not to a copier trading after the
   30-45-day disclosure lag. A narrow, testable version exists (see
   §Congressional below) and is scoped as a backtest-first design, but
   the literature prior is negative and I recommend against spending on
   it this cycle.
3. The two forward paper tests already running (corrected momentum
   scanner, wheel) remain the highest-value use of the account:
   genuinely out-of-sample data accruing at zero marginal cost.

## 1. Social sentiment (Reddit / Twitter / StockTwits)

**What credible sources say:**

- An arXiv backtesting study (July 2025, "Backtesting Sentiment Signals
  for Trading", arxiv.org/pdf/2507.03350) applied FinBERT-class models
  to news from AccessWire, Benzinga, Reuters, Yahoo Finance and
  concluded the strategies **struggle to generate alpha once
  transaction costs are included** — positive paper returns, eroded or
  eliminated in implementable form.
- The CFA Institute digest of the StockTwits/Twitter literature
  ("Momentum, Mean-Reversion, and Social Media") reports that highly
  abnormal social sentiment is **preceded** by high momentum and
  **followed by mean reversion** — i.e., by the time sentiment is
  extreme, the move has happened. The tradable content overlaps with
  price momentum we already tested and killed.
- The canonical cautionary tale predates all of this: the 2011
  Bollen "Twitter mood" paper spawned Derwent Capital's sentiment hedge
  fund, which closed within months. The pattern — strong in-sample
  paper result, failed live implementation — is 15 years old.
- Reddit/WSB event studies (Bradley et al. and successors) find
  measurable moves around viral DD posts, but concentrated in the
  2020-21 meme regime, requiring entry within minutes of posting
  (a latency race retail loses), and decaying sharply after the effect
  was publicized — textbook alpha decay.

**Quality of the positive claims:** the "Sharpe 5.0 Reddit portfolio"
and "2-5% annual outperformance" figures surfaced by search come from
promotional/aspirational sources (vendor blogs, Medium posts, 2-year
cost-free backtests on hand-picked tickers). None survive the
standards this repo applies to its own results.

**Practical blockers even if an edge existed:** historical Reddit data
(Pushshift) is no longer freely available and the Twitter/X API is
paywalled — an honest backtest can't even be sourced cheaply, and the
decision gate forbids building a live scraper before a backtest proves
the edge.

**Decision: closed.** Same failure mode as price-action rules — any
signal is either already in the price or dies to costs and latency.

## 2. News-sentiment momentum

Covered by the same arXiv 2507.03350 result (news feeds, FinBERT,
costs kill it) and by the information-transfer literature finding
public news is impounded into prices too fast for slow retail reaction.
LLM-augmented variants (several 2025-26 arXiv papers) report in-sample
improvements but share the same untested-costs, short-sample,
no-out-of-sample-protocol weaknesses. **Closed.**

## 3. Congressional trading disclosures

The one place the literature documents real (not aspirational)
abnormal returns — with a fatal catch for copiers.

**What the peer-reviewed record actually shows:**

| Study | Period | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Ziobrowski et al. (2004, 2011) | 1993-1998 Senate / 1985-2001 House | Mimicking portfolios beat market ~12%/yr (Senate) / ~6%/yr (House) — **pre-disclosure era, not copyable in real time** |
| Eggers & Hainmueller, "Capitol Losses" (SSRN 1762019) | 2004-2008 | No informed trading; average member would have done **better in an index fund** |
| Belmont et al., NBER w26975 ("Senators As Feckless As the Rest of Us") | 2012-2020 | Senators' buys **underperform** industry peers 11-28bp; no committee-linked picking skill; lone anomaly = post-COVID-briefing sells |
| J. Public Economics 2022 (S0047272722000044) | post-STOCK Act | Aggregate alpha **disappeared after the 2012 STOCK Act**; House buys underperform ~26bp/6mo |
| "Captain Gains on Capitol Hill", NBER w34524 (Nov 2025) | through 2025 | Abnormal returns concentrated in **chamber/party leaders** after ascending to leadership, via political-influence + corporate-access channels. (Magnitude in the fetched abstract summary looks implausibly large and is unverified against the full paper.) |

**Why this doesn't translate into a retail strategy:** the copyability
chain has three decay stages, each documented:
(1) the average member has **no** alpha post-2012, so copying "Congress"
wholesale copies noise with negative drift on buys;
(2) what alpha exists is concentrated in a handful of leaders' trades
around discrete events (regulatory actions, contract awards) — exactly
the trades whose value decays fastest;
(3) the STOCK Act allows up to **45 days** between trade and public
disclosure (median lag ~4-6 weeks, late filings common, $200 penalty),
and the post-disclosure event-study evidence for followers is
somewhere between nothing and noise. The copy-trading products (Quiver,
Unusual Whales, NANC/KRUZ ETFs) are conflicted sources; NANC's 2024
outperformance is a mega-cap tech tilt (beta + concentration), not
demonstrated disclosure alpha — KRUZ, same construction, lagged badly.

**The narrow, testable version (scoped, NOT built):** if this is ever
pursued, the only defensible design is:
- **Hypothesis (pre-registered):** purchases disclosed by chamber/party
  leadership and relevant-committee chairs, in policy-sensitive
  sectors, earn positive abnormal returns over 1-13 weeks measured
  **from the public disclosure date** (not the trade date).
- **Data:** point-in-time PTR filings with actual filing timestamps
  from the official House/Senate clerk archives (reconstructable back
  to ~2013; this is the hard part — vendor CSVs backfill trade dates,
  which silently reintroduces lookahead).
- **Test:** event study vs. industry-matched benchmarks, 2bp/side
  costs, then the same rolling walk-forward protocol as everything
  else in this repo. No live scraper, no orders, until that passes.
- **Prior: negative.** Every non-conflicted post-2012 study points the
  wrong way; this design exists to falsify cheaply, not because the
  evidence favors it.

## 4. Reality-check on the wheel (search #4)

Independent practitioner backtests corroborate this repo's own honesty
section: spintwig's SPY wheel studies find **none of ten wheel variants
beat buy-and-hold SPY** on total return, and in the 45-DTE 5-delta
variant **>99% of the return is attributable to the long SPY exposure**,
not option premium. The wheel currently paper trading should be
expected to deliver roughly T-bill-to-modest returns with equity-grade
drawdowns — income-shaped beta, not alpha. The forward paper test stays
(it costs nothing and measures our implementation), but nobody should
mistake it for an edge engine.

## 5. "What works for retail in 2026" (search #5)

The search surface is dominated by content marketing (indicator
listicles, invented statistics). Filtering to defensible claims, three
things have evidence behind them, none of which is a new bot to build
tonight: (a) **cost/execution hygiene** (already done: commission-free
paper, costs modeled); (b) **slow factor premia** — value/momentum/
quality tilts at monthly+ horizons with decades of out-of-sample
evidence, which is portfolio construction, not trading; (c) **narrow
domain specialization** in venues where sophistication hasn't
concentrated yet (e.g. prediction markets) — a research direction, not
a buildable system with documented parameters.

---

## Decision

- **Build nothing this cycle.** Sentiment/news momentum fails the
  decision gate explicitly: no credible documented edge after realistic
  costs. Congressional copying fails it on the evidence (post-STOCK-Act
  alpha for followers is undocumented outside conflicted vendors); the
  narrow leadership-subset event study above is the only version worth
  ever testing, is backtest-first by design, and is deliberately left
  unbuilt.
- **Keep accruing forward paper data** on the corrected momentum
  scanner and the wheel — the only genuinely out-of-sample tests
  running.
- Anything that revives a closed hypothesis space (price-action
  variants, sentiment overlays on price signals) requires a new
  pre-registered justification per `WALKFORWARD_RESULTS.md`.
